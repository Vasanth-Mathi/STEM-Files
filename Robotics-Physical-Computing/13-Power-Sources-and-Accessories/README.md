# Power Sources and Accessories

> **Source note:** Component names, basic purpose, connection guidance, and example uses are based on the uploaded **Microcontroller Boards Reference** document. Short student explanations are added to make the material easier to learn. Exact pin labels, voltage limits, polarity, and module variants must be checked on the actual component before wiring.

Power components provide electrical energy to the project. Voltage, polarity, current capability, connector type, and load requirements must all match.

| No. | Item | How It Works | How to Use / Connect | Example Use |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 75 | Rechargeable 9V Battery | Stores chemical energy and supplies DC voltage. | Connect positive and negative to suitable project power inputs. Check whether the battery can supply the required current. | Portable low-power projects |
| 76 | 9V Battery Charger | Recharges a compatible rechargeable 9V battery. | Insert/connect only the battery type supported by the charger and follow manufacturer instructions. | Battery charging |
| 77 | 9V Battery Snapper | Clips onto a 9V battery and provides two wires. | Match positive and negative leads correctly. | Battery connection |
| 78 | 9V Battery Clip with DC Jack | Converts the battery connection to a barrel/DC plug. | Connect battery, then plug into a compatible device input. Verify polarity. | Portable Arduino power |
| 79 | 5000/10000 mAh Power Bank | Stores energy and provides regulated USB power. | Connect a USB cable from the power bank to a USB-powered device. | Portable USB power |

## Power Rule
Do not assume one battery is suitable for every project. Motors and other high-current loads may need a different supply from the controller.
