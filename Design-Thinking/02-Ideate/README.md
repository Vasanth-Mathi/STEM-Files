# Ideate

## What This Stage Means

Ideate means generating **many possible ways of responding to a clearly defined problem**.

The purpose is not to find one perfect answer immediately. Students first explore possibilities before judging them.

```text
Defined Problem
      |
      v
Many Possibilities
      |
      v
Compare Possibilities
      |
      v
Select Ideas to Explore
```

## Why Ideation Matters

People often choose the first idea that comes to mind. Design Thinking deliberately delays that decision.

A wider set of ideas helps students:

- avoid obvious assumptions,
- consider different users,
- explore low-cost approaches,
- combine ideas,
- consider non-technical possibilities,
- notice constraints earlier.

## Rules for Early Ideation

During the first idea-generation stage:

- aim for quantity,
- do not criticise ideas immediately,
- record unusual ideas,
- build on another person's idea,
- include simple ideas,
- include low-cost ideas,
- include non-digital ideas,
- include ideas that improve a process, not only a product.

## Useful Ideation Questions

- What else could happen here?
- Can the task be reduced?
- Can a step be removed?
- Can the user receive information earlier?
- Can the experience become easier to understand?
- Can the task be done with less physical effort?
- Can the process work for more kinds of users?
- Can two ideas be combined?
- What would a very low-cost version look like?
- What would work even without electricity or internet?

## Idea Selection

After generating several possibilities, students can compare them using criteria such as:

- usefulness,
- accessibility,
- safety,
- cost,
- time,
- materials available,
- technical difficulty,
- maintenance,
- environmental impact.

Students should justify their choice rather than choosing only the most impressive-looking idea.

## Student Evidence

Keep:

- original problem statement,
- brainstorm sheet,
- sketches,
- at least five different ideas,
- idea-combination notes,
- selection criteria,
- comparison table or decision matrix,
- reason for choosing the idea to prototype.

## Check Before Moving Forward

- Did we generate several genuinely different ideas?
- Did we avoid choosing too early?
- Did the selected idea respond to the user's need?
- Did we consider constraints?
- Can we explain why this idea is worth testing?
