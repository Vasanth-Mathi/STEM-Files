# Reflect

## What This Stage Means

Reflection is the stage where students examine **what they learned from the complete process**.

Reflection is not only a summary of what they built.

It should explain how understanding changed from the beginning to the end.

```text
Original Assumption
       |
       v
Research + Ideas + Prototype + Testing
       |
       v
New Understanding
       |
       v
Reflection + Next Step
```

## What Students Reflect On

### The User
- What did we misunderstand at first?
- What did we learn about the person's real need?
- Which evidence changed our thinking?

### The Problem
- Did the original problem statement change?
- Was the problem more complex or simpler than expected?
- Did we focus on the correct part of the problem?

### The Idea
- Why did we choose this direction?
- Which alternative idea might still be useful?
- Which assumption was weakest?

### The Prototype
- What did Version 1 reveal?
- What changed in Version 2?
- Which change was based on evidence?

### The Evaluation
- Which success criteria were met?
- Which were not met?
- What feedback repeated across users or trials?

### Personal Learning
- What did I personally contribute?
- Which skill improved?
- What mistake taught me something useful?
- What would I do differently next time?

## Limitations

A strong reflection openly states limitations.

Examples include:

- small number of users,
- limited testing time,
- unavailable materials,
- unrealistic prototype conditions,
- cost not yet checked,
- only one environment tested,
- accessibility not tested with enough users.

## Final Reflection Structure

Students can write:

1. **What we believed at the beginning**
2. **What evidence we collected**
3. **What changed in our understanding**
4. **What we built or tested**
5. **What succeeded**
6. **What failed**
7. **What we improved**
8. **What limitation remains**
9. **What we would investigate next**
10. **What I personally learned**

## Important

Reflection can send the team back to any earlier stage.

```text
Reflect ---> Empathise / Define / Ideate / Prototype / Evaluate
```

Design Thinking is iterative, not a one-way checklist.
