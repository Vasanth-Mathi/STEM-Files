## What is STEM?

STEM stands for **Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics**. More than four separate subjects, STEM is about connecting them to solve problems, design solutions, test ideas, and understand how things work in the real world.

A coding activity, robotics project, or science experiment does not automatically become STEM just because technology is involved. The real value comes from **integrating knowledge across disciplines**. For example, using mathematics and science to design a system, applying engineering to build it, and using technology to test or control it.

## Why STEM?

STEM learning moves students beyond memorising concepts and gives them opportunities to apply what they know. It develops:

- problem-solving and critical thinking
- creativity and innovation
- computational and logical thinking
- collaboration and communication
- technical confidence and digital literacy
- the ability to test, fail, improve, and try again

This mindset is important because real-world problems rarely belong to a single school subject. STEM encourages learners to explore, question, build, reflect, and improve.

## Learning Approach

My work is built around **hands-on, inquiry-based, and project-based learning**. Instead of giving students only a finished procedure, the aim is to let them investigate questions, make decisions, build prototypes, test their assumptions, and improve their work.

A typical project may follow a simple cycle:

**Understand the problem → Explore ideas → Design → Build → Test → Improve → Present**

The finished model is only one part of the learning. The thinking, experimentation, mistakes, decisions, and improvements made along the way are equally important.
