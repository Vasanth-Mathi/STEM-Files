# Design Thinking Problem Coach Prompt

## Purpose
Guide students from a vague problem idea toward user evidence, a focused problem statement, alternatives, and a test plan.

## What Students Learn
- Empathy and observation
- Separating needs from assumptions
- Problem definition
- Ideation
- Decision criteria
- Prototyping and testing

## Detailed Prompt
```text
Act as a design-thinking coach. Do not jump directly to a solution.

Grade: [enter]
School / community context: [enter]
Problem I noticed: [enter]
People affected: [enter]
Evidence I already have: [observations / interview notes / survey / none]

Guide me through these stages:

EMPATHISE
- Ask what I observed directly.
- Ask what users said or did.
- Separate evidence from my assumptions.
- Suggest safe, respectful questions I could ask users.

DEFINE
- Help me write: "[user] needs a way to [need] because [evidence/insight]."
- Help me create 3 measurable success criteria.
- Identify constraints such as time, cost, materials, accessibility, safety, and privacy.

IDEATE
- Make me generate at least 5 ideas before judging them.
- Ask me to include one simple idea, one unusual idea, and one low-cost idea.
- Do not choose for me.

SELECT
- Help me create a decision matrix using 4-5 criteria.
- Ask me to score and justify my own choice.

PROTOTYPE
- Suggest the lowest-cost prototype that can test the most important assumption.

TEST
- Create 5 observation questions for testing.
- Ask what evidence would show success or failure.
- Require me to record what changed between Version 1 and Version 2.

Never invent user interviews, survey results, quotations, or test data.
```

## How to Use
Use real observations whenever possible. The prompt should support the process, not replace user research.

## Student Evidence
Keep observation notes, problem statement, idea sheet, decision matrix, prototype evidence, test data, and V1/V2 changes.

## Reflection Questions
- Which assumption was wrong?
- What evidence changed your design?
- What limitation remains?

## Responsible AI Check
AI is not the user. Real people affected by the problem should inform the design.
